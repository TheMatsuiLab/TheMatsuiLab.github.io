About the lab

Nanotechnology built for medicine

The Matsui Laboratory works where nanotechnology meets medicine. After engineering peptide and protein assemblies, biomimetic autonomous motors, enzyme-mimicking peptides and inorganic nanocages, the lab now focuses on medical applications of nanotechnology: nanoparticle-based drug delivery systems, medical imaging and lab-on-a-chip cancer diagnostic devices.

Two programs lead our current work: cage-shaped iron oxide nanoparticles that carry drugs and RNA into cells, and synthetic peptides that amplify the production of therapeutic exosomes and extracellular vesicles.

The lab is based in the Belfer Research Building, where Hunter College scientists work side by side with colleagues from Weill Cornell Medicine.

Research areas

What we work on

All research

Iron oxide nanocages

Cage-shaped iron oxide nanoparticles, under 20 nm, that carry anticancer drugs or therapeutic RNA inside their cavity.

Magnetic gene delivery

In alternating magnetic fields, nanocages move by Brownian relaxation and help their cargo escape endosomes to deliver siRNA.

Exosome engineering

Self-assembling synthetic peptides that amplify the production of extracellular vesicles from therapeutic cells.

RNA therapeutics

RNA delivery for gene therapy and gene editing, including nanocage delivery of engineered RNA constructs in cancer models.

Medical imaging

Superparamagnetic nanocages can act as MRI contrast agents, so drug delivery can be monitored in vivo.

Cancer diagnostics

Impedance-based sensors that detect cancer cells through changes in their mechanical properties.
